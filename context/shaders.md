# shaders/*.wgsl

WGSL shaders sharing `Transform4D` (`rotation_matrix`, `viewer_distance`, `sticker_scale`, `face_gap`, `face_gap_4d`, `elapsed_seconds`), `CameraUniform`, and `StickerInstance` structs plus 4D math functions (and `inverse3`, generic enough to sit alongside them), all defined once in `math4d.wgsl` and pulled into each pipeline shader via `naga_oil`'s `#import` (composed in `renderer.rs` through a `naga_oil::compose::Composer`, since WGSL itself has no import mechanism). `sticker_common.wgsl` similarly shares `LightUniform`, `HighlightingUniform`, the `piece_slots` storage array, and the `sticker_order` storage array (and their bound globals) across every sticker material shader and the particle pipeline; its `@group(0) @binding` numbers are ordered by descending readership across the classic/elemental/particle pipelines rather than by which module owns the declaration. `classic_shader.wgsl` is the Classic theme's sticker material (flat per-`kind` colors from a `KindColors` uniform); `elemental_shader.wgsl` is the Elemental theme's, switching on `kind` to pick an animated material per element (helpers `hash11`/`hash21`/`hash31`/`value_noise2`/`value_noise3`/`strobe` shared via `elemental_common.wgsl`, which also holds `LIGHTNING_STROBE_HZ` and `ICE_TWINKLE_HZ` so each element's material animation and its particles stay on one beat) and driven by `Transform4D.elapsed_seconds`. Fire, Light and Dirt are the exceptions to that switch: `fs_main` discards `kind == 3` (Fire), `kind == 4` (Dirt) and `kind == 5` (Light), each shaded instead by its own fragment entry point over the same `vs_main` - `fs_fire`/`fs_dirt`/`fs_light` - which `renderer.rs`'s `fire_pipeline`/`dirt_pipeline`/`light_pipeline` draw per sticker in a blended, depth-write-off pass, depth-sorted together with Ice via `shader_widget::depth_draw_order`/`DepthLayer` (see `context/renderer.md`). Rather than shading a surface, `fs_fire` raymarches a small flame cube inset in the sticker's own cube: `compute_sticker_anchor` supplies the cube's world center and its three warped half-edges, `math4d::inverse3` turns those into a map back into the sticker's local frame, and the ray from `CameraUniform.eye_position` through the fragment is marched there against an axis-aligned box (`ray_box`) - so the flame comes out stretched in world space exactly as its facet is, and the inside/outside test stays an L∞ (Chebyshev) `max(abs(x), abs(y), abs(z))` rather than a Euclidean `length()`. `FIRE_STEPS` jittered samples accumulate `sun_palette` emission against a transmittance (early-out below 0.01) and the result is returned premultiplied, as `vec4(emission, 1 - transmittance)`. A 4D rotation can squash a facet flat, collapsing that frame toward a plane and sending its inverse to infinity, so `fs_fire` discards below `FIRE_MIN_FRAME_VOLUME` - the frame's own volume as a fraction of the volume its edge lengths would span if perpendicular. The field itself is `solar_plasma`: two rotations plus a -y drift of the noise domain (both in the local frame, so "up" turns with the puzzle) feeding `ridged_warp` (2 octaves) into `ridged_detail` (3 octaves), all built on `elemental_common`'s `value_noise3`/`hash31` - a `fract`-folded hash rather than the `sin`-based `hash11`/`hash21`, since a volume sampled thousands of times per pixel drives a `sin` hash's argument far enough out to alias into regular banding. The palette's stops run far above 1.0 and carry `FIRE_EXPOSURE`, which is why the theme's composite tonemaps. Every fragment of the cube on one pixel yields the same ray, so `fs_fire` discards its own back faces by their current `world_normal` (rather than by `cull_mode`, which stays `None` everywhere, since `world_normal` - unlike index winding - is derived fresh from the instance's own basis and never goes stale mid-move) to keep from marching the flame twice and blending it over itself. `fs_light` mirrors this raymarching structure - the same `compute_sticker_anchor`/`inverse3` local frame, `ray_box` march, and degenerate-frame/missed-box/back-face discards - but with a boxed 3-octave-FBM cloud density (`light_cloud_density`, built on `elemental_common::value_noise3` rather than a second noise primitive) in place of Fire's `solar_plasma`, self-shadowed by a short sub-march toward `sticker_common::light.direction` and shaded with a fixed warm/ambient palette; unlike Dark, it still reads as lit by the scene's own light rather than being fully self-illuminated. `fs_dirt` shares Fire's exact local-frame setup (`compute_sticker_anchor`/`inverse3`/`ray_box`) but marches a hard SDF (`dirt_map`, a box minus an fbm rock bump, ported from a standalone source) instead of accumulating a density, so its coverage is always a binary 0.0 (a `discard`, on a missed box, a failed march, a degenerate frame, or a back face) or 1.0 (a shaded hit) rather than Fire's/Light's continuous transmittance; seeded by `piece_slot`, like Fire, so a piece's rock pattern stays with it across a move. Every entry point routes its result through `apply_highlight`, whose `coverage` argument scales the tint the same way the color it is mixed into is scaled, so the hover tint reads the same on Fire's/Light's premultiplied output as on an opaque material. `fs_fire`'s reads of `camera` and `instances` made `binding: 1` and `binding: 2` fragment-visible in `main_bind_group_layout`; `fs_light` and `fs_dirt` read the same bindings, already fragment-visible. `particle_shader.wgsl` draws the Elemental theme's billboarded particles: a per-`kind` count of camera-facing quads per sticker (`Theme::particles_per_kind`, renderer.rs), generated entirely in the vertex shader from `@builtin(vertex_index)`/`@builtin(instance_index)` with no vertex buffer and no stored particle state - `sticker_index` is looked up from `sticker_common::sticker_order`, an indirection buffer grouping instances into contiguous `(face_id, kind)` blocks so `renderer.rs` can issue one draw per visible face and populated kind at that kind's own budget. `vs_main` switches on `kind` to dispatch to one function per element (`ice_particle`, `fire_particle`, etc.), each returning a `ParticleSample` (world position, billboard size, color, alpha) for `vs_main` to place; every element but Fire is a thin wrapper building its own `ParticleStyle` and handing it to the shared `styled_particle`, which hashes `instance_index` against the current lifetime cycle for a particle's launch direction and age, so the whole system is a function of `elapsed_seconds` with all distances expressed in sticker radii. `math4d.wgsl`'s `compute_sticker_anchor` supplies the sticker's projected world center plus the three half-edge vectors of its projected cube; particle directions are drawn evenly over the unit sphere in that local frame and mapped through those edges, so travel distance and billboard size follow however the 4D projection has warped that particular sticker - unequally per axis, not by one shared scale.

`gizmo_shader.wgsl` is a trimmed `debug_shader.wgsl` for the rotation-axis gizmo (see `context/renderer.md`/`context/shader_widget.md`): it imports only `math4d::CameraUniform` and takes a plain per-vertex `position`/`color` pair (no per-instance storage buffer, unlike `debug_shader.wgsl`'s `DebugInstance` array), since the gizmo's ring/marker geometry is already fully resolved to 3D positions on the CPU each frame and its coloring varies within one draw call rather than per instance.

`post_process.wgsl` holds the HDR bloom chain `renderer.rs`'s `composite()` runs after every other pipeline has drawn into the offscreen `scene_view`: `vs_fullscreen` (a fullscreen triangle from `@builtin(vertex_index)` alone) is shared by `fs_bright_pass` (thresholds `scene_view` into a half-res target), `fs_blur_h`/`fs_blur_v` (a separable 9-tap Gaussian blur, one pass per axis), and two composite variants over a shared `composite_color` (which adds the blurred result back into `scene_view`, bilinearly upsampled for free by `textureSample` against a half-res source): `fs_composite` writes that sum straight out, while `fs_composite_tonemapped` first runs it through `aces_tonemap` (Narkowicz's curve fit), which `renderer.rs` selects under `Theme::Elemental` so the emission its materials write above 1.0 rolls off along a shoulder instead of clipping flat at the surface format's range. The first three read the vertex shader's own `uv` output, resolution-independent by construction, since their render pass is never viewport-restricted; only `fs_composite`'s is (to `Renderer::bounds`, a sub-rectangle of a larger `scene_texture`), so it instead derives its UV from `@builtin(position)` divided by `scene_texture`'s full `textureDimensions`, giving the absolute address a renormalized 0..1 UV over just the visible sub-rectangle couldn't.
