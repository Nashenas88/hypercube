# shader_widget.rs

The custom iced `shader::Program`/`Primitive` that owns essentially all rendering and interaction state, independent of `HypercubeApp`. `HypercubeShaderProgram` (per-frame config), `HypercubeShaderState` (persistent: camera, 4D rotation matrix, hover/click/double-click bookkeeping, animation state, the `Hypercube` puzzle state), `HypercubePrimitive` (per-draw snapshot). Mouse/keyboard events are handled here; a `RotateButton` setting assigns one mouse button to camera orbit (+Shift for 4D rotation) and the other to puzzle turn-clicks, so the two never conflict. A turn-click's direction is resolved by `moves::clockwise_sign` to always turn clockwise as viewed along the clicked facet's own rotation axis; Shift reverses it to counterclockwise. Double-click on a face triggers a "center this face" animation via `math::shortest_arc_plane`; a 3D camera-orbit drag doesn't interrupt it, but a Shift+drag (which writes `rotation_4d` directly) takes over and cancels it the moment the drag starts. A reveal/hide flourish (`AnimatingReveal`) spins the camera 720° in yaw while sticker scale/face gap sweep toward secondary/primary defaults, driven by the same `RedrawRequested`-tick loop as the move/focus animations; camera-drag and turn-click input are ignored while it plays.

`draw()` also builds `HypercubePrimitive::depth_order` under `Theme::Elemental` (empty otherwise): `depth_draw_order` collects the instance indices of `ELEMENTAL_FIRE_KIND`/`ELEMENTAL_ICE_KIND`/`ELEMENTAL_LIGHT_KIND` stickers on visible faces, tags each `DepthLayer::Fire`/`Ice`/`Light`, and topologically layers them into farthest-first batches (`Renderer::render()`'s per-batch passes, see `renderer.md`) - all three need this because what they draw depends on draw order, whether from blending (Fire, Light) or from a batch's shared background snapshot needing every farther batch's result already in it (Ice). `FireSticker::is_behind` decides each pair by ray casting: for each of a sticker's 8 real projected corners (`math::transform_sticker_vertices_to_3d`, the same per-vertex transform the vertex shader and hover/click picking use), it casts a ray from the camera eye through that corner and tests it against the other sticker's actual triangle geometry with `ray_casting::ray_sticker_intersection` (prefiltered by `ray_casting::ray_intersects_aabb` against a precomputed `ray_casting::AABB`) - the same exact ray-vs-cube test hover and click picking already use, rather than an approximating scalar key. A nearer hit means the other sticker occludes that corner; a farther hit means the corner is nearer. This is done with both stickers' corners tested against each other, since a size mismatch after the 4D perspective divide can leave every corner of a smaller sticker outside a larger one's silhouette even while they visually overlap; when tested corners disagree - which can happen for two stickers on different tesseract cells whose shared boundary is viewed near-edge-on, since that boundary is no longer a single plane after each cell's own projection - the verdict from whichever corner point is nearest the camera wins. A pair whose corners never land inside each other's geometry (the common case; most fire stickers don't overlap on screen at all) yields no edge, so `topological_draw_order` linearizes only the edges that exist into an order, taking the farthest currently-unblocked sticker at each step (which alone reduces to plain depth order wherever there are no edges) and degrading to depth order for anything caught in a cycle. While a move animation is sweeping a slab the face's cells are no longer the axis-aligned lattice the rest of the frame assumes, so `draw()` passes `lattice_is_aligned: false` and depth alone decides for every pair.

`draw()` also computes `HypercubePrimitive::ui_controls.ground_truth_debug_face` via `ground_truth_debug_face()`, for `Renderer`'s Fire ground-truth debug inset (see `renderer.md`): `None` when the debug toggle is off, the theme isn't `Elemental`, or nothing is currently kind `ELEMENTAL_FIRE_KIND`; otherwise it cycles through every face_id currently holding a Fire sticker, ascending, spending `GROUND_TRUTH_DEBUG_FACE_CYCLE_SECONDS` on each before advancing and looping forever. It's driven by `elapsed_seconds` rather than its own timer, since Elemental's continuous materials already force a redraw every tick.

`HypercubeShaderState.elapsed_seconds` accumulates real wall-clock time across `RedrawRequested` ticks (wrapped modulo 3600), independent of `last_redraw_instant` (which resets to `None` once no animation needs another tick) via its own `last_tick_instant`, and is threaded through `HypercubePrimitive`/`prepare()` into `Renderer::update_instances`. Selecting `Theme::Elemental` forces that tick's `event::Status` to `Captured` even when every other animation is idle, since Elemental's sticker materials animate continuously off `elapsed_seconds` alone and have nothing else to signal that a redraw is still needed.

`HypercubeShaderState` also owns a `rng: fastrand::Rng` and a `random_moves_generation` counter that mirrors `reset_generation`'s mismatch-detection pattern in `Program::update()`: on a mismatch it applies `random_move_count` random legal moves instantly via `Hypercube::apply_random_moves`, bypassing `AnimatingMove` entirely - backing the UI's random-move/Scramble buttons.

A `reset_generation` mismatch leaves the puzzle's piece arrangement untouched and only resets the 4D orientation, animating it back to identity rather than snapping it: `AnimatingReset` decomposes `rotation_4d` (via `math::decompose_so4`) into a pair of unit quaternions at the moment Reset is pressed and slerps both toward identity each tick, recomposing `rotation_4d` (via `math::compose_so4`) - the true geodesic path in `SO(4)`, unlike the single-plane `AnimatingFocus` approach. While it plays, 4D drag rotation, double-click "center this face", and turn-clicks are all no-ops; 3D camera drag is unaffected.

`save_generation`/`load_generation` mismatches drive explicit puzzle save/load, the same generation-counter bridge `reset_generation` uses - necessary because `HypercubeApp` has no direct handle on the live `Hypercube`, which lives only in `HypercubeShaderState`. This module never touches `puzzle_state.rs`/disk itself: on a save mismatch it clones `state.hypercube` and hands it up via `Action::publish(Message::PuzzleReadyToSave(..))` for `HypercubeApp` to persist; on a load mismatch it takes an already-loaded `Hypercube` out of `HypercubeShaderProgram::pending_load` (a `Cell<Option<Hypercube>>`, since `Program::update` only gets `&self`) - populated by `HypercubeApp` calling `puzzle_state::load()` before threading it in - and, if present, replaces `state.hypercube` and clears the same in-flight animation/interaction fields a reset does. A `None` `pending_load` (missing or invalid save file) is a no-op.

A `save_snapshot_generation` mismatch (debug-only Save Snapshot button) follows the same bridge shape but needs no round trip through `HypercubeApp`: `Program::update()` builds a `snapshot::ViewSnapshot` directly from `self`'s per-frame config and `state`'s live puzzle/camera/rotation, stores it in `state.pending_snapshot` (a `Cell<Option<ViewSnapshot>>`, same reason `pending_load` is one), and syncs the counter. `draw()` takes it into `HypercubePrimitive::snapshot_request`; `Primitive::prepare()` - which has `device`/`queue`, unlike `render()` - captures this frame's actual pixels via `Renderer::capture_frame` and calls `snapshot::save` when it's `Some`, so the saved PNG matches what's on screen exactly rather than a separately-reconstructed render.

**Rotation-axis gizmo.** An opaque, shaded ring torus plus small marker toruses with directional arrows, drawn to make the 4D plane a rotation is happening around legible: the click-to-focus animation (`AnimatingFocus`), a live Shift+drag 4D camera rotation, and the Reset animation (`AnimatingReset`) - not `AnimatingMove` (a puzzle turn), which has no meaningful single 4D rotation plane at all. `build_gizmo_vertices` first checks `HypercubeShaderProgram::show_gizmo_ring` (threaded from `HypercubeApp`'s "Show Gizmo Ring" checkbox, off by default) and returns no vertices at all when it's off, before considering any of the three animation sources below. Focus and drag each have a single well-defined rotation plane; Reset's accumulated orientation is generally a 4D "double rotation" with two independent invariant planes rather than one, so its ring comes from the free function `reset_plane_and_phase`, which reuses `combined_drag_plane_and_phase`'s generator-combination pattern (below) applied to `AnimatingReset::start_p`/`start_q`'s `scaled_axis()`es instead of drag deltas - exact in the same structural cases that function is exact in, an approximation (a single "characteristic" ring) for a fully generic double rotation. Because slerping a quaternion toward identity preserves its axis and only shrinks its angle, the plane is derived once from the animation's start quaternions and the phase angle alone scales down with progress (`reset_phase_at`), rather than being recomputed every tick. The ring lies in the rotation's **invariant plane** (`math::orthogonal_complement_plane` of the rotation's own plane), not the plane that's actually sweeping, so it never warps mid-animation - only the puzzle's rendered orientation moves around it. A live drag is tracked by `active_shift_drag: Option<ActiveShiftDrag>`, accumulating the signed angle each of `process_4d_rotation`'s two independent component rotations (horizontal: camera-right/W plane; vertical: camera-up/W plane) has applied since the gesture began (via `math::mouse_delta_to_plane_angles`, kept in sync with `process_4d_rotation`'s own sensitivity math); it's created lazily on the first Shift+drag mouse-move and cleared on button release, Shift release, or a reset animation taking over `rotation_4d`.

A live drag's invariant plane necessarily contains the camera's own forward axis - a provable, unavoidable fact of `process_4d_rotation`'s basis (`{camera_right, camera_up, camera_forward, w_axis}` is a complete orthonormal 4-basis, so the complement of `(camera_right, w_axis)` is *exactly* `span(camera_up, camera_forward)`, and symmetrically for the vertical component), not a bug - so a flat ribbon built in that plane would collapse to a literal zero-width line from every camera/puzzle orientation. `gizmo_torus_vertices` avoids this by giving the ring real 3D thickness (a torus, not a ribbon), so it always presents a visible cross-section regardless of viewing angle. `HypercubeShaderProgram::build_gizmo_vertices` (called from `draw()`) picks a source - `animating_focus`'s plane if present, else the free function `combined_drag_plane_and_phase`, which combines `active_shift_drag`'s two independent component rotations into the single ring "whose angle is determined by the relative motion of the two original rings": to first order, applying both simultaneously is exactly equivalent to one rotation about `span(normalize(h*right + v*up), w)` by angle `sqrt(h² + v²)` (their generators add linearly since `right_4d ⊥ up_4d`), degenerating cleanly to the old single-axis case when one component is ~0 and hidden entirely (returning `None`) once that combined angle is still under `GIZMO_MIN_DRAG_ANGLE`; else, while `animating_reset` is in progress, `reset_plane_and_phase`, following the same shape (see above). Any source is handed to `gizmo_torus_vertices`, which samples points around the invariant plane's unit circle and projects them with `math::project_4d_to_3d` using an **identity** rotation, never the puzzle's live `rotation_4d`: the plane vectors are already expressed in the same display-space frame the perspective divide expects directly (`AnimatingFocus::plane` comes from `rotation_4d`-multiplied face normals; a drag's comes from the camera's own basis), so multiplying by `rotation_4d` again would double-apply whatever orientation the puzzle had when the animation/drag began, making the ring visibly drift/warp as that separate rotation composes on top of the very sweep it's meant to hold still against.

The main ring's per-major-angle frame (`radial`/`binormal`, recomputed from scratch at each sampled angle rather than propagated, so the tube closes with zero seam/twist) sweeps a small tube cross-section (`GIZMO_RING_TUBE_MINOR_RADIUS`/`GIZMO_RING_TUBE_SEGMENTS`) colored in `GIZMO_BAND_COUNT` (4) alternating-hue bands (`GIZMO_FOCUS_PALETTE`/`GIZMO_DRAG_PALETTE`/`GIZMO_RESET_PALETTE`) that depend only on the cross-section's own minor angle and `phase_angle`, not the major angle - so the whole tube reads as spinning in place about its own centerline in sync with accumulated rotation progress, freezing the instant the rotation does. `GIZMO_MARKER_COUNT` (4) small field-loop marker toruses - "like the loops of a magnetic field around a current-carrying wire" - sit at fixed, equidistant positions along the ring (they never travel around it), each built directly in the already-projected 3D space perpendicular to the main ring's own tangent at that point (the outer positioning step still needs finite-differencing two nearby projected points; each marker's own small-circle frame is then exact closed-form, no epsilon needed) and given a flat, non-banded `marker_color`. Each marker carries `GIZMO_MARKER_ARROW_COUNT` (3) small solid 3D arrow cones evenly spaced around *its own* circumference, creeping only around that local loop as `phase_angle` advances - `phase_angle` being the focus animation's eased angle, the drag's combined angle, or the reset's shrinking combined angle, so the arrows only creep as actual rotation progress advances, never off wall-clock time alone, always pointing tangentially in the actual direction of rotation (reversing if it reverses) and freezing the instant the rotation itself does. Each marker's own flat color is deliberately distinct from the main ring's own band colors (`GIZMO_FOCUS_MARKER_COLOR`/`GIZMO_DRAG_MARKER_COLOR`/`GIZMO_RESET_MARKER_COLOR`) so it's clear which tube is which where they cross. The ring and marker tubes get a true smooth per-vertex normal computed analytically as they're built (the unit radial direction from a tube's own centerline at that point - already known in closed form, no neighbor lookups needed), so their curved surfaces read as round rather than faceted; each arrow cone instead gets a CPU-computed flat per-triangle face normal (`recompute_flat_normals`, run once per marker's own arrow-cone vertices), matching its intentionally low-poly look. The result becomes `HypercubePrimitive::gizmo_vertices`, uploaded by `Renderer::update_gizmo` and drawn inside `Renderer::render` itself, in the same pass as the opaque hypercube and before the translucent Fire/Ice/Light/Dirt batches (see `renderer.md`).

**Tutorial interaction reporting.** `HypercubeShaderState` tracks five one-shot flags (`tutorial_orbited_reported`/`tutorial_rotated_4d_reported`/`tutorial_turned_face_reported`/`tutorial_turned_other_piece_reported`/`tutorial_focused_face_reported`) and publishes the matching `Message::TutorialOrbited`/`TutorialRotated4d`/`TutorialTurnedFace`/`TutorialTurnedOtherPiece`/`TutorialFocusedFace` the first time each interaction happens this session - a plain 3D camera-orbit drag, a Shift+drag 4D rotation (a separate, later tutorial step from plain orbit, so it's a distinct message), a facet click that starts a move (`TutorialTurnedOtherPiece` instead, once `TutorialTurnedFace` has already fired, when the clicked facet's piece has 3+ stickers - see `FacetGeometry::facet_count()` in `context/piece.md`), or a double-click that starts a focus animation - so `app.rs`'s first-run tutorial can unlock a step's "Next"/"Done" button only once the user has actually tried what that step describes (see `app.md`). Each flag latches after its first report so a multi-tick drag doesn't republish the same message every frame; the pending message rides in `tutorial_event`, drained at the end of `update()` behind a completed reveal's message and a pending solve message (same one-message-per-call constraint `solve_outbox` exists for).

**Tutorial piece-type restriction/flash.** `HypercubeApp` computes, from its current `tutorial_step`, which facet count (if any) input should be restricted to and which one or two facet counts (if any) should be pulse-highlighted, and hands both down as `HypercubeShaderProgram` fields (`tutorial_restrict_facet_count: Option<u8>`, `tutorial_flash_facet_counts: [u8; 2]`) - this module stays agnostic of which tutorial step means what, consuming only "restrict to X"/"flash A and B". `find_intersected_sticker` (`ray_casting.rs`) takes `tutorial_restrict_facet_count` and excludes any facet whose `facet_count()` doesn't match from being hoverable, which transitively keeps it unclickable too, since `handle_facet_click` only ever acts on `state.hovered_sticker`. `tutorial_flash_facet_counts` rides through `HypercubePrimitive` into `Renderer::update_tutorial_flash`, which drives a wall-clock pulse in both pipeline shaders (see `context/shaders.md`).

**Solve playback.** A `solve_command_generation` mismatch (Puzzle → Solve / Stop Solving, carrying a `SolveCommand` payload the way `random_move_count` rides with `random_moves_generation`) either stops playback (`Stop`: the queue and outbox are dropped; the move in flight still finishes) or runs `solver::solve` synchronously on the live `Hypercube` (`Start`, a few ms) and publishes `Message::SolveProgress`/`SolveEnded` (`AlreadySolved`, or `Failed` with the `SolveError`, which is also `log::warn!`ed). A non-empty solution becomes a `SolvePlayback` queue; its first move starts at once unless a turn-click's move is still animating. Each move is applied atomically like a click and animated as an ordinary `AnimatingMove`, but over the fixed `SOLVE_MOVE_DURATION` (40 ms) rather than `animation_duration_ms`. In the `RedrawRequested` branch, as soon as `advance_animation` completes a move, `advance_solve_playback` starts the next one in the same tick - its `elapsed` starting from `move_overshoot` (how far the previous move ran past its duration, capped at one move) so frame timing doesn't slow playback - or, with the queue empty, ends playback with `SolveEnded { Completed }`. Those messages go through `solve_outbox`, published at the end of `update()` unless a reveal-complete message is published that call (a published message makes iced request another redraw, so the solve's goes out next call). Every message carries the starting generation so `HypercubeApp` can drop late ones from a cancelled run. Reset, Random Moves and a successful Load cancel playback; a Reveal lets it continue. While it plays, turn-clicks and hover picking are off (camera and 4D drag still work).
